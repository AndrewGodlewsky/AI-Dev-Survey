## Q1 — Does v1 ever run on the team, or does v2 replace it first? *(prerequisite — the rest of the round hangs on this)*

### What this is

Whether 5–9 people answer v1 in the next few weeks, or whether v1 ends its life as a thing only you ever took.

### Why it matters

It decides whether item text is **frozen**. If the team answers v1, every item is a live database key: v2 must either preserve the wording or accept that the two runs can never be compared, and the v2 work waits behind a real run. If nobody else answers v1, the keys are free and v2 can rewrite anything at no cost.

It also decides **what v2 is designed against**. Right now every finding is one person's read — yours. A real run would tell you which item pairs never diverge, whether N5 gets guarded answers when a manager is genuinely downstream, and whether the Team block feels redundant to people who didn't design it.

### Options

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Replace** — v2 before anyone else answers | Nothing is wasted on a block you already know is redundant; item keys are free; v2 ships once. | v2 is designed on n=1. Every structural call rests on your read alone. |
| **B. Run v1 first, then v2 on real data** | v2 gets evidence: real divergence, real timing, real guarded answers. v1 is built and free to run. | Spends the team's goodwill on a survey you already want to change; a second ask in a few months lands worse. |
| **C. Run v1 on a subset (2–3 more people), then v2** | Some evidence, most goodwill preserved. | Still too small for divergence stats; costs a cycle. |

### My recommendation

**A — replace.** Your two survey findings are about *how it felt to answer*, and that's precisely what a pilot exists to surface; running 5–9 people through a block you've already established is redundant spends real goodwill to confirm a known result. But say it explicitly — if you'd rather have data first, the map changes shape entirely and the dashboard work goes first while v1 runs.

### Your answer

: V2 is going to be a complete replacement. We're not going to be using V1. I just built it and was testing it, and these are the changes that I want to implement before I really roll this out to the team.

## Q2 — Does `scoring/scoring.xlsx` survive, or does Python take over scoring entirely?

### What this is

You said the *paste* was clunky and the dashboard's look was fine. Two very different fixes answer that.

### Why it matters

It decides whether the scoring rules live in **one place or two**, and how much working, verified work gets deleted. It also touches two standing constraints in `CLAUDE.md`: *Excel: formulas only, no macros/VBA*, and *Dashboard: one self-contained HTML file, opened from disk, no external CDNs*. Both were written for v1; a Python app reverses the second and may retire the first.

### Options

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Python automates the paste** — reads the Forms export, writes the Scored CSV, dashboard unchanged | Smallest diff. Excel sheet and its 5,224 verified formulas keep working. Scored CSV contract untouched. | Keeps Excel in the loop — the exact step you called clunky is still there, just scripted. Two implementations of the scoring rules to keep in sync. |
| **B. Python replaces the sheet** — reads the export, computes scores in code, serves the dashboard | Scoring rules live in exactly one place. The clunky step is gone, not automated. `scoring.xlsx` and `make-scoring-xlsx.mjs` are deleted. | Deletes real, verified work. Drops the "formulas only" constraint. Breaks RUBRIC's promise that a human can hand-score from the sheet. |
| **C. Both** — Python is the normal path, Excel kept as a fallback | Belt and braces. | The drift problem in its worst form: two engines, one rarely exercised, silently diverging. |

### My recommendation

**B.** Keeping Excel in the loop preserves the thing you actually complained about, and a spec plus 5,224 formulas will drift the first time a rule changes. The hand-scoring promise survives in the **Rubric prose** — that's a document, not a spreadsheet, and `RUBRIC.md` already carries the full procedure and a worked example. I'm flagging this loudly rather than assuming it, because B throws away a ticket's worth of finished, acceptance-tested work.

## Q3 — Does the Team section still score the same seven Dimensions?

### What this is

The sharpest question on the board, and I don't think your answer is settled yet. You wrote that the Team section should be *"more about development practices as a team and the future of multiple developers developing in parallel."* That is not the same seven Dimensions asked in a team frame — it's a **different construct**.

### Why it matters

The **Stance gap** is the one thing this survey measures that a generic AI-readiness questionnaire doesn't: *"I'd go further than the team should"* and its reverse. It's only a real quantity when both stances answer the same item. Replace the Team block with genuinely different questions and the Stance gap doesn't degrade — it ceases to exist, along with the dashboard's Stance slice, the norm gap, and about a third of the Scored CSV.

The redundancy you felt is real either way. The question is whether the cure is *fewer of the same* or *something else entirely*.

### Options

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Same Dimensions, fewer and better-worded items** | Stance gap fully intact. Cheapest change. Scored CSV barely moves. | Most likely to still feel like "the same questions again" — structurally, it is. |
| **B. Same Dimensions on a subset** — keep only the 3–4 where "I would" vs "the team should" genuinely diverges; drop the rest | Big row saving. Stance gap survives where it's meaningful. Redundancy drops sharply. | Which 3–4? At n=1 there's no divergence evidence, so the pick is judgment. Partial Stance gap needs explaining in the Rubric and one-pager. |
| **C. A genuinely new team-practices block** — parallel dev, handoffs, shared conventions, AI review norms | It's what you described. Opens a topic v1 never asked about. Nothing feels repeated. | Stance gap dies. v2 becomes a different instrument, not an overhaul. Needs a new Dimension set, new rubric levels, new scoring — a large chunk of the v1 map re-walked. |
| **D. B + C** — a slimmed stance block *and* a small new team-practices section | Keeps the headline finding and buys the new topic. | Longest of the options in rows; needs both a subset pick and a new construct designed. |

### My recommendation

**B**, and I'll argue against **C** even though C is closest to what you wrote. The Stance gap is the finding that starts an actual conversation in a team of 5–9; C trades it away to buy a topic that D could buy *alongside* a slimmed stance block. If the redundancy you felt was really about *answering 30 rows again* rather than *the questions themselves*, B fixes it and C is overkill.

If you want C anyway that's a legitimate call — but then v2's destination must say "a different instrument", and the map gets meaningfully longer.

### Your answer

: I don't think the team section can still score on the same seven dimensions, because ultimately I think I'm looking for different questions there to be more team-oriented. We might have to come up with some alternate dimensions that the team would be scored upon more, along the lines of how principles at a team level should work and how developers will need to interact with AI while interacting with other developers in the future.

## Q4 — Does the team Coverage pass (section 10 — 19 rows, "Team in 6 months") survive?

### What this is

Independent of Q3. You could cut the 30 mirrored Likert rows and still ask the team where each of the 19 Development areas should land — or cut this too. It's 19 answers, the same grid for the third time, and it feeds the Coverage norm gap.

### Why it matters

Three passes over one 19-row grid is the most mechanical stretch of the survey. By the third pass people are pattern-matching against their own previous answers rather than answering — which is exactly the failure mode [Straight-lining](https://github.com/AndrewGodlewsky/AI-Dev-Survey/issues/20)'s Pattern flags were built to catch, except Coverage is deliberately excluded from those flags.

### Options

| Option | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Cut it** — two passes: today, and me in six months | Saves 19 answers. Keeps the personal Coverage Gap, the one that drives individual conversations. | No team Coverage norm — you lose "where should *we* be on security review". |
| **B. Keep all three passes** | Full Coverage picture including the team norm. | The most mechanical 19 rows in the survey, answered third. |
| **C. Keep a reduced team pass** — team norm on only the six high-stakes areas | Keeps the norm where it matters (the areas that already raise Edge markers at level 6). | A third grid with different rows to explain; asymmetric Coverage data. |

### My recommendation

**A — cut it.** Two passes keep the Gap that drives individual conversations, and the third pass is where fatigue is worst. If Q3 lands on **C** or **D**, cutting this becomes near-automatic; if it lands on **A** or **B**, it's still the cheapest 19 rows on the board.

### Your answer

: No, this does not survive. I don't want the team section that's going to be completely revamped. I don't want to have a section that's in six months. I just want to have a section about what it should be or what each person wants it to be, not necessarily where it is now and where it is in six months. That's more of the self-assessment thing that I really liked, so we're going to cut it for sure.
